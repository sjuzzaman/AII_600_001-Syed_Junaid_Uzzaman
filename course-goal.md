# Goal of AII 600

**AII 600 — Foundations and Practice of Machine Learning for Artificial Intelligence** (a first-year grad course at GMU) is built on one core premise:

> **An AI assistant can produce an analysis, so a take-home analysis by itself is no longer evidence that a student understands anything.**

So the course's goal isn't to test whether students can *produce* correct code/output (an AI can do that for them) — it's to build and verify two things:

1. **Understanding** — can a student explain the mechanism behind an analysis, interpret it in context, and predict what changes if an assumption changes, *without* AI help (tested live: midterm, defense).
2. **Judgment** — can they state the purpose of an analysis, compare reasonable alternatives, weigh the cost of errors, calibrate claims to evidence, and defend/revise a decision under questioning.

## Structure

- **Weeks 1–7**: foundations (probability → estimation → regression/classification → decisions → model selection → deep learning), reinforced by low-stakes self-check labs (AI allowed).
- **Week 8 midterm**: pen-and-paper, no AI — the individual check that understanding actually transferred.
- **Weeks 9–15**: team project work with AI fully allowed, but every studio page and the final defense forces students to justify decisions, name rejected alternatives, and identify what evidence would change their mind — plus a cumulative decision log.
- **Grading** (50/40/10 = Understanding/Judgment/Preparation) reflects this: it explicitly rewards being able to defend and reason about work, not just having correct-looking output.

In short: the course treats "AI can write the analysis" as the given, and grades students on the judgment and understanding needed to be accountable for it.
